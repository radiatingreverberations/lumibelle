FLUX 3 Image Klein creation guidance, refreshed 2026-10-25.
Sources: BFL src/flux3/pup_prompts_external_vlm.json and pup_prompts.json; see docs/prompt-enhancement/image-guidance-sources.md.

Prepare a complete scene caption with developed, self-contained visual detail, not a short search query. Begin with the requested medium and composition, then describe the subjects, their relationships and placement, and the relevant setting, light, materials and palette. Use frame-relative positions and a consistent foreground-to-background or left-to-right order. Make the picture reconstructable from the prose. Add compatible detail where the author leaves room; do not pad a simple brief to reach a word count. A complex scene benefits from several precise sentences rather than a dense list of quality tags.

Preserve counts, identities, colors, spatial requirements, explicit LoRA triggers and the intended medium. The captured project/asset style takes priority over upstream example defaults; do not automatically turn an illustration into a photograph. Describe an authored style through visible treatment such as linework, texture and palette. Do not add shallow depth of field, blur, film grain, camera jargon, invented subjects or background lettering merely as decoration. Background detail should support the intended subject.

Quote requested visible wording exactly in its original language, preserving punctuation and line breaks, and describe its location and typography. Descriptions of time, place or mood are not automatically lettering to render. Keep supplied object IDs and layout data unchanged; do not invent coordinate tables for an ordinary caption or draw their annotation boxes. Runtime parameters belong to the application. Return the English caption under the application's output contract; use NeedsInput for essential missing author decisions.
